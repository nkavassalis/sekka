# Prescription Refills — Routing SOP
> HYPOTHETICAL EXAMPLE POLICY — fictional practice and pharmacy rules.

## Intake (every request, every word)
Patient name+DOB · medication · dose · prescriber · pharmacy name & phone ·
request date · "controlled? Y/N (if unknown, check chart)".

## Routing
- **Non-controlled**: task to prescriber queue `RX-NONCTRL`; decision due
  within 72 business hours; patient told "call pharmacy after 2 p.m. in
  three days".
- **Controlled (C-II)**: no early refills, pharmacy must fax the prescriber,
  ID copy at pharmacy per state rule (fictional here: form RX-ID).
- **Controlled (C-III–V)**: one early refill allowed per 6 months if lost/
  stolen with a police report number; otherwise route as C-II.
- **Early refill, no qualifying reason**: prescriber decision only — front
  desk never says yes, and never says "no" either: "the doctor will call
  you back".

## Windows
- Refill requests received after 15:00 are dated next business day.
- Travel/vacation overrides: form **RX-TRV**, prescriber approval, once/year.
- After-hours line handles nothing about refills — patients call the
  answering service, staff never queue work for off-hours.

## Escalate to prescriber immediately
Therapy-gap > 3 days on an essential med · patient reports no drug strength
matches · any "I ran out early" involving sedatives or opioids.
